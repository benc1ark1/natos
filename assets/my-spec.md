# My Personal AI Dashboard

## 1. What I actually want

I want one place where I can put everything I need to do, remember, study, practise or think about, without having to organise it manually every time.

The dashboard should answer three questions immediately:

**What should I do today?**

**What is coming up that I might forget?**

**What should I be working on over the next few days or weeks?**

I do not want productivity itself to become another task.

Ideally, I should be able to type naturally:

"Need to watch the CS hash-table video before Wednesday."

"I want to do an SMC session sometime this weekend."

"Need to revise mechanics next week."

"I haven't trained football in a few days."

"Physics homework due Friday."

...and the AI should understand what each thing is, remember it, organise it and bring it back at the right time.

---

# 2. The main problem I had with Notion

My problem was not that Notion lacked features.

It had **too many things I had to manage myself**.

I ended up thinking about: databases, properties, relations, projects, areas, recurrence, dates, statuses, calendar views, filters, templates and where each item belonged.

Instead of simply doing my work, I was maintaining the system.

Another major problem was consistency. If I forgot to enter something properly, the whole system became less useful.

Recurring tasks also created clutter. Things like morning routines, gym sessions or daily habits could generate loads of old entries and make the dashboard messy.

My schedule also changes. I do not want every activity assigned to an exact time.

Something like: "Do an SMC session Saturday or Sunday" is useful. Having to decide "SMC: Saturday 14:00–15:30" when I do not actually know what Saturday will look like creates unnecessary friction.

I also kept rebuilding and redesigning Notion instead of simply using it.

The system therefore needs to be **low maintenance, forgiving and AI-managed**.

---

# 3. The basic philosophy

The system should be **capture first, organise second**.

I should not need to decide whether something is a task, a project, a habit, a goal, a routine, a note, a resource or a calendar event before entering it.

I tell the AI what is happening. The AI determines what it means.

My job should mostly be: **capture → look at dashboard → do work → mark complete** rather than constantly administering the system.

Football is particularly important because some sessions are flexible. For example: "I want four football sessions this week." That does not mean I necessarily want four fixed calendar events. The dashboard should know that I have completed two and still want another two.

## PERSONAL

Everything else that matters but does not need to dominate the main screen: reading, philosophy, things I want to learn, music/media analysis, personal projects, volunteering, admin, family/friends commitments, routines, planning, books/articles/videos I want to return to, general reminders.

**I do not want seven giant categories visible every day.** Study / Athletic / Personal should be enough for the main interface.

# 8. Calendar

The calendar should mainly contain things that genuinely happen at a particular time: school, CS tutor Mon + Thu, maths tutor Wed (can change week to week), appointments, matches, training sessions with a fixed start time, events, deadlines where the time genuinely matters.

School from roughly 08:45–16:00 on weekdays belongs on the calendar as one repeating commitment. It should **not create five separate daily "go to school" tasks**.

The calendar answers: **When am I busy?** The task system answers: **What do I need to get done?** They should work together but remain conceptually different.

# 9. Schoolwork

The system needs to be particularly good at school because I have several overlapping academic goals: Maths, Further Maths, Physics, Computer Science independently, SMC preparation, competition/problem-solving work.

I often discover tasks in different ways. A teacher may give homework. I might realise during a question that I need to revise a topic. My CS tutor may give me a video. I might decide I want to practise a particular SMC technique. All of these should be captured in the same place.

I should be able to tell the AI: "I still don't understand vectors properly." and it can keep that as something to revisit rather than requiring me to build an entire "Vectors project."

# 10. SMC

The dashboard should know my larger goal while still turning it into concrete sessions. Instead of showing **Project: SMC preparation** it should show **Tonight: finish/review 2020 Q11–16** or **Weekend: one timed paper + review mistakes**.

The project exists in the background. The dashboard surfaces the **next action**.

# 15. AI planning

I should be able to ask "What should I do tonight?" and it should consider: deadlines, what I have already completed, school commitments, my academic priorities, football/gym frequency, tasks I have been postponing, approximately how much time is available, and what is urgent versus merely important.

Then give me a realistic plan. Not "Here are 17 things you could consider." Something like:

**Tonight**
1. Physics homework — 35 min
2. CS video — 30 min
3. SMC questions — 45 min
4. Football touches — optional 25 min

# 22. Ideal interaction

The system should feel almost like having a personal chief of staff. I tell it things throughout the day. It remembers them. It notices deadlines. It keeps track of recurring goals. It knows what I have completed. It knows what I have ignored. It brings things back when they become relevant.

Then I can ask: "What matters today?" "What have I forgotten?" "What should I do after school?" "What schoolwork is coming up?" "How am I doing with football this week?" # 23. Ideal dashboard layout

- **TODAY**: the 3–6 things that deserve my attention.
- **UPCOMING**: important deadlines and commitments over roughly the next 7–14 days.
- **THIS WEEK**: progress toward flexible goals such as football, gym, SMC and other ongoing work.
- **CALENDAR**: actual fixed commitments.
- **QUICK CAPTURE**: one box where I can dump anything in normal language.
- **ASK AI**: a persistent assistant that understands all the information above.

Everything else — projects, resources, history, notes, categories and metadata — can exist underneath the interface without constantly being visible.

# 24. The core rule

The dashboard should **reduce the number of decisions I have to make about managing my life**. The system should organise the information. I should spend my energy deciding what I actually want to accomplish and then doing it.

The test for the whole system is simple: if adding a task feels harder than just trying to remember it, the system has failed. If I can dump something into the AI in five seconds and trust that it will resurface at the correct time, the system is working.
